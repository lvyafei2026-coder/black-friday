When Is Black Friday in Canada? The Canadian Version of the Day, Explained

Black Friday in Canada lands on the same Friday as in the United States — the day after American Thanksgiving, which puts it somewhere between November 23 and November 29. Canada's own Thanksgiving falls in October, so the date is set entirely by the U.S. calendar. What differs north of the border is timing: the hour deals go live, which retailers join in, and how the weekend stacks up against Boxing Day.

Why Canada's Black Friday Follows the U.S. Calendar

Canadian Thanksgiving is celebrated on the second Monday of October, roughly six weeks before the American version. That gap is why Black Friday in Canada has no domestic holiday anchor. Retailers here adopted the U.S. event and simply mirror its date, so the sale always arrives on the fourth Friday of November, the morning after American Thanksgiving.

Because the date is imported, it shifts every year. The earliest it can fall is November 23 and the latest is November 29, and the weekend around it can stretch into December when the holiday lands late.

None of this makes the day a statutory holiday in Canada. Federal offices, banks, schools and most workplaces run a normal Friday schedule, and no province applies holiday shopping restrictions. Stores stay open, and many malls extend their hours for the weekend.

What Time Do Canadian Black Friday Deals Start?

Online sales are usually scheduled against Eastern Time, since most Canadian retailers are headquartered in Ontario or Quebec. A deal listed as starting at 12:00 a.m. Friday is therefore 9:00 p.m. Thursday in Vancouver, 10:00 p.m. in Calgary and Edmonton, and 11:00 p.m. in Winnipeg and Regina.

East of Ontario the clock runs the other way. In Halifax and the rest of Atlantic Canada, midnight Eastern is 1:00 a.m. local. Newfoundland, on its half-hour offset, sees the same launch at 1:30 a.m. Shoppers there are effectively browsing a sale that opened ninety minutes earlier by their own clocks.

In-store timing is looser. Door deals depend on each mall's opening hours, and queue times vary by city and by how deep the discount is. Store hours are usually posted a week or two ahead on the retailer's own site.

Which Canadian Retailers Take Part

Most large Canadian chains now run some version of the event: Amazon.ca, Best Buy, Walmart Canada, Canadian Tire, Hudson's Bay, Simons, Sport Chek, Staples and Indigo among them. Many open their deals on the Monday or Tuesday before the Friday and keep them running through Cyber Monday.

Grocery and pharmacy chains tend to treat it as a flyer week rather than a single day. Loblaws, Metro, Shoppers Drug Mart and similar stores usually publish week-long pricing, with the flyer resetting on a Thursday or Friday depending on the chain.

Smaller independent shops are the least predictable. Some run one-day discounts, others skip the event entirely and put their markdowns into Boxing Day instead. Checking a store's social feed a few days ahead is usually more reliable than any listing.

Black Friday vs. Boxing Day in Canada

Boxing Day on December 26 was the traditional Canadian clearance day long before Black Friday crossed the border, and it remains a statutory holiday in Ontario. For televisions, laptops, winter apparel and general inventory clearing, it still produces some of the year's deepest cuts.

Black Friday has grown quickly, particularly online, where it now rivals Boxing Day for traffic. Whether it beats December 26 depends on the category and the retailer — electronics appear in both windows, while furniture and seasonal stock are more often cleared after Christmas.

The practical difference is return windows. Anything bought in late November usually falls under extended holiday return policies, so a Black Friday purchase and a Boxing Day purchase of the same item often carry the same deadline in January.

Returns, Price Adjustments and Cross-Border Orders

Canadian return policies commonly stretch over the holidays, with purchases made from early November onward accepted into the second or third week of January. The exact cutoff is set by each retailer, and some exclude large appliances, opened software or final-sale items. Confirming the window before checkout is worth the minute it takes.

Buying from a U.S. site is a separate calculation. Cross-border orders can attract duty, federal and provincial sales tax, and a courier brokerage fee on delivery, which can erase the saving on smaller items. Several large U.S. retailers now ship from Canadian warehouses, so the same product may arrive with no border charges at all.

Price adjustments are another variable. Some Canadian chains will refund the difference if an item drops in price within a set number of days, but most exclude door-crasher and limited-quantity deals. Ask at the counter rather than assuming the policy applies.

FAQ

Is Black Friday a statutory holiday in Canada?

No. It is not a federal or provincial statutory holiday, so banks, offices and schools generally keep their normal Friday schedule while retailers stay open.

Is Black Friday in Canada the same day as in the United States?

Yes. It falls the day after American Thanksgiving, which means the Friday always lands between November 23 and November 29.

Do Canadian Black Friday sales start before the Friday?

Often. Many retailers open their deals earlier in the same week and keep them running through Cyber Monday. The exact start time depends on the store.

Does Amazon Canada run Black Friday deals?

Yes. Amazon.ca typically hosts a multi-day event around the date, with offers rotating daily rather than appearing all at once.

Is Boxing Day bigger than Black Friday in Canada?

It depends on the category and retailer. Boxing Day on December 26 remains the traditional clearance day, while Black Friday now draws comparable volume online.
