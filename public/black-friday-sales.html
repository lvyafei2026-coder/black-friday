Black Friday Sales Don't Start at Once — Here's How They Roll Out

Black Friday sales rarely appear all in one morning. Retailers publish ad scans weeks ahead, switch on online pricing at different hours, and keep some discounts for stores only. The hard part usually isn't finding a sale — it's telling a genuine markdown from a repackaged regular price. Here's where the announcements come from, what the big-name discounts actually look like, and how to check a deal before you pay.

Where Sale Announcements Come From

Most large chains publish their Black Friday ad scan in late October or early November, usually a few weeks before the sale itself. The scan is the most detailed source you'll get: it lists doorbuster items, per-store quantities, and the hours each price applies. Deal sites and newspapers often post copies early, sometimes before the retailer's own page goes live.

Fashion, beauty, and accessory brands frequently skip the printed circular. They announce sales through email lists and text alerts, and subscribers generally get access a day or two before the public. If you already know which brand you want, joining its list is usually faster than watching for a leak.

Third-party ad aggregators vary a lot in quality. Check whether a posted scan names the current year and cites where it came from — a scan with no date is often a leftover from a previous season, and the prices in it may never have been real.

What Big-Name Discounts Actually Look Like

Walmart's sale typically combines an ad scan with an earlier online start and in-store doorbusters on the day. Doorbuster quantities are limited per store, and popular items are often gone before general opening hours. Online orders placed for pickup are usually priced at the online rate, which can differ from the shelf price.

Brand sales such as Michael Kors usually take a percentage off selected styles rather than the whole site. Exclusions commonly cover new arrivals, certain collaborations, and gift cards, and the outlet site generally runs a different discount from the main store. Read the exclusions line before you plan a purchase around a headline percentage.

Across most stores, the deepest cuts land on older models, seasonal stock, and house brands. Current-generation phones, new game consoles, and full-price designer pieces rarely drop much, no matter what the circular suggests.

How to Judge a Discount Before You Pay

Compare the sale price against the recent street price, not the list price printed next to it. A "was" figure can refer to a price almost nobody paid. Price-history tools show what an item actually sold for over the past 30, 60, or 90 days, which is a better benchmark than the crossed-out number.

Check the model number or SKU carefully. Retailers sometimes stock configurations built for the sale — a TV with fewer ports, a laptop with less memory — under a name that looks nearly identical to a better-spec model. Search the exact SKU before assuming you've found the same product cheaper.

Bundles, gift cards, and store credit inflate the advertised value without lowering what you pay for the item you want. Work out the price of the single product you're after, then decide whether the extras are things you'd buy anyway.

Online Versus In-Store Sales

The same retailer can run different prices online and in its stores on the same weekend. Doorbusters are often store-only, while web-only deals may not be honored at the register. If a price matters, check both channels before you leave home.

Online sales generally switch on earlier in the week and stay live longer than the in-store event. Store hours on the day itself vary by chain and location, and some retailers open earlier than the hours listed on their national site — individual store pages are more reliable.

Buy-online-pickup-in-store is the usual middle path. You lock in the online price and skip the queue, though availability is per store and popular items sell out quickly. Place the order as soon as the price goes live rather than waiting for the day.

Returns and Price Adjustments on Sale Purchases

Many retailers extend their return window for purchases made from late October or November through the end of the year, but the exact dates and eligible categories differ by chain. Marketplace sellers and third-party listings on the same site often follow different rules from the retailer itself.

Price adjustments — refunding the difference if an item you bought gets cheaper — usually apply within a short window of about one to two weeks, and doorbusters and flash deals are commonly excluded. It's worth asking at the counter or in chat before you buy, since policies change year to year.

Clearance, final-sale, and large-appliance items are frequently excluded from both returns and adjustments. Keep a copy of the ad scan or a screenshot of the price you saw; some stores ask for proof when the discount has already ended.

FAQ

When do Black Friday ad scans come out?

Most large retailers release them in late October or early November, typically a few weeks before the sale. Smaller brands often skip the scan and announce discounts by email instead.

Are Black Friday sales actually cheaper?

It depends on the item. Older models and house brands usually drop meaningfully, while new releases and full-price designer goods often don't. Comparing against recent street prices rather than the listed original price is the safer check.

Does Walmart publish a Black Friday ad?

Yes, Walmart typically publishes an ad scan ahead of the event, with online pricing starting earlier than the in-store doorbusters. Quantities on doorbuster items are limited per store.

Does Michael Kors run Black Friday sales?

Michael Kors usually discounts selected styles rather than the full site, with exclusions on new arrivals and some categories. The outlet site runs a separate promotion from the main store.

Can I get a price adjustment if something I bought goes on sale?

Often, but only within a short window of roughly one to two weeks and usually not on doorbusters or clearance. Policies vary by retailer, so confirm before you buy.
