Is It Black Friday Today? Here's How to Tell

Black Friday is not a fixed date on the calendar — it is pinned to Thanksgiving, which is why the day shifts every year. That means the only reliable way to answer the question is to check where today falls against that rule. Here is how to work it out in seconds, plus why so many stores seem to be running the sale already.

The Rule That Sets the Date

Black Friday is the Friday immediately after Thanksgiving in the United States. Thanksgiving is the fourth Thursday of November, so Black Friday always lands on a Friday between November 23 and November 29. If today is a Friday inside that window, it is Black Friday. If today is a Friday in any other month, it is not — no matter what a banner claims.

The reverse check helps too. If today is a Thursday in November and it happens to be the fourth one, then tomorrow is the day. If today is a Wednesday or a Saturday in late November, the day has either not arrived yet or already passed.

Because the holiday is tied to a weekday rather than a number, the date moves by a few days each year. That is why people look it up instead of remembering it.

Why Deals Show Up Before the Day

Retailers rarely wait for the date itself. Many launch promotions in late October or early November and label them "early Black Friday," "Black Friday week," or simply "Black Friday sale." Seeing a discount does not mean the day has arrived — it means a store chose to start its campaign early, and those campaigns have grown longer over time.

To judge whether a sale is the real thing, look at the end date rather than the wording. A promotion that runs for several weeks is a seasonal campaign, not a single-day event. A countdown or an end time close to the holiday is a stronger signal that the offer is tied to the actual date.

This matters most when you are deciding whether to buy now or wait. Early offers are sometimes the best of the season, but they are not guaranteed to be, so comparing before you commit is usually worth the extra step.

The Day Starts at Different Times

Midnight is not a single moment for everyone. Online sales commonly open at midnight in the retailer's home time zone, which frequently means the US East Coast. A shopper in Europe or Asia will therefore see prices change at a different hour of their own day.

Physical stores set their own opening hours, and some choose to open very early or stay open late. Those schedules are decided by each retailer and vary widely, so the store's own page is the only dependable source.

If a deal page says the sale is live but your local calendar still shows the day before, both can be true at once. Check the timestamp and the time zone the retailer is using.

Does the Rest of the World Follow the Same Date?

The event comes from American Thanksgiving, so most retailers outside the US simply adopt the US date rather than calculating their own. Shoppers in the UK, Europe, and much of Asia generally see promotions on the same Friday.

There are exceptions. Canada's own Thanksgiving falls in October, but Canadian retailers usually still run Black Friday in November alongside the US. Some markets also hold separate regional sales events on other dates, which can look similar but are not the same day.

When you shop a store based outside the US, check whether its timing follows the American date or a local schedule. A quick look at the campaign's start and end times usually settles it.

If Today Isn't the Day Yet

There is still useful work to do before the date arrives. Save the items you want, note their current prices, and set a reminder for the week of the holiday so you can compare the discounted price against the original one rather than against a crossed-out figure.

It also helps to decide in advance what you actually need. Sales periods are designed to encourage quick decisions, and a short list made beforehand keeps the day from turning into browsing.

Some retailers repeat or extend their best offers, but that varies by store and by product, so it is not something to count on. Treat early prices as information, not as a guarantee of what the day itself will bring.

FAQ

Is it Black Friday today?

Only if today is the Friday directly after the fourth Thursday of November. Any other Friday, or any other weekday, is not Black Friday itself.

Why do I see Black Friday deals before the date?

Many retailers launch promotions days or weeks early and label them "early Black Friday." A sale banner shows that a campaign is running, not that the day has arrived.

What time do Black Friday deals start?

It depends on the retailer. Online sales commonly open at midnight in the store's home time zone, while physical stores set their own opening hours.

Do other countries have Black Friday on the same day?

Most international retailers follow the US date, since the event is tied to American Thanksgiving. A few run their own regional sales on different days.

Can Black Friday fall in October?

No. The US Black Friday is always in November, between the 23rd and the 29th. October promotions are early sales, not the day itself.
