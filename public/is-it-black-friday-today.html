Black Friday Countdown

Is It Black Friday Today?

Black Friday is the Friday after Thanksgiving, so the answer changes from year to year. If today is a Friday that falls between November 23 and November 29, then today is Black Friday. If it isn't, the countdown shows how many days are left until the next one.

How to Tell If Today Is Black Friday

There is one rule to remember: Black Friday is the day after Thanksgiving, and Thanksgiving is the fourth Thursday of November. Count the Thursdays in November, take the fourth one, and the Friday that follows is Black Friday. No other Friday in the year carries the name, so a Friday in October or December is never Black Friday.

Because the rule is tied to a weekday rather than a fixed date, the answer changes every year and is no on almost every day of the calendar. It is yes for a single day, once every twelve months. Early sales, previews, and extended return windows all happen around that date, but none of them move it.

The possible window is narrow: Black Friday can only fall between November 23 and November 29. The earliest date, November 23, happens when November 1 is a Thursday. The latest, November 29, happens when November 1 is a Friday. Anything outside that range is a different sale under a similar name.

When the Next Black Friday Falls

The countdown on this page runs to the next Black Friday, so you don't have to work the date out by hand. Once this year's Friday has passed, the timer rolls over to the following year. Time is counted in full 24-hour periods, which is why the final day often shows hours rather than a round number.

If you are planning around the date, note that the shopping week begins before the Friday itself. Many retailers open promotions earlier in the week, and some run member-only previews. The Friday is the busiest day, but it is rarely the first day a discount appears.

The weekend that follows leads into Cyber Monday, when online-only offers tend to show up, and travel deals often cluster around the Tuesday after that. None of these shift the date of Black Friday itself, but they explain why the discount season feels longer than a single day.

Black Friday Dates Through 2029

Using the fourth-Thursday rule, the upcoming dates are: 2026 — November 27; 2027 — November 26; 2028 — November 24; 2029 — November 23. Each falls on a Friday. Years beyond this list follow the same rule, so you can work them out yourself if you need a date further ahead.

The date drifts by a few days from one year to the next, then jumps back. That pattern comes from the mismatch between a 365-day calendar and a seven-day week, with leap years nudging the sequence. It is the same reason Thanksgiving moves each year while fixed-date holidays such as Christmas do not.

What Goes on Sale on Black Friday

Black Friday started as an in-store retail event and is now largely an online one. Electronics, appliances, clothing, and household goods are the categories that usually see the deepest discounts, with toys and gaming gear close behind. How deep a discount goes depends on the retailer and the year, so a strong category last year is no promise for this one.

Prices are not always at their lowest on the Friday itself. Some items are cheaper in the weeks before, and some drop further during post-holiday clearance. Comparing a price with its usual level, rather than with an advertised original price, is the more reliable way to judge a deal.

Stock is the other variable. Popular items can sell out within hours of a promotion opening, and restocks are not guaranteed. If a specific model matters to you, checking availability before the day is more useful than waiting for a bigger percentage off.

Black Friday Outside the United States

The date is American in origin, but the name is used widely elsewhere. Retailers in the UK, Canada, Ireland, Australia, and parts of Europe run promotions on the same Friday, largely because online stores do not want to sit out a major shopping day. In most of those markets the sales are shorter and the discounts shallower than in the US.

Some countries have their own discount traditions that overlap with it, such as Singles' Day in China and Boxing Day in the UK and Commonwealth countries. If you are shopping from outside the US, the Friday date stays the same, but shipping, duties, and return policies depend on where the store ships from.

Common Misconceptions

People often assume Black Friday is a fixed date. It is not, and it never has been. Others assume it always lands in the last week of November, which is close but not exact: it can appear as early as November 23, which is not the last week in every year.

Another common mix-up is treating the whole sale period as Black Friday. Discounts that begin in early November or continue into December belong to the surrounding season, not to the day itself. The date is still one Friday, and it is the one the countdown is measuring.

FAQ

Is today Black Friday?

Only if today is a Friday between November 23 and November 29. Otherwise the next Black Friday is the date shown in the countdown.

When is Black Friday this year?

It falls on the Friday after the fourth Thursday of November, which places it somewhere between November 23 and November 29.

Is Black Friday always on a Friday?

Yes. The name comes from the day itself, which is the Friday after Thanksgiving in the United States.

Do Black Friday sales start before Friday?

Often they do. Many retailers open promotions earlier in the week and some offer member previews, so the exact start depends on the store.

Is Black Friday the same date in the UK?

UK retailers generally use the same Friday as the US, since the date follows the American Thanksgiving calendar. Sales there tend to be shorter.
