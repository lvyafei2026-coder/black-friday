Is It Black Friday Today?

Black Friday is defined by where it sits in the calendar, not by a fixed date, so the answer changes from year to year. You can settle it with two quick checks: the day has to be a Friday, and it has to fall the day after Thanksgiving in the United States. If both are true, today is Black Friday. If not, you are either inside the run-up to it or already past it.

Two Checks That Answer It

Black Friday is always the day after Thanksgiving, and Thanksgiving in the US is the fourth Thursday of November. That means the test is simple: is today a Friday, and was yesterday the fourth Thursday of the month? If yesterday was a Thursday falling between November 22 and November 28, today is Black Friday. If today is a Friday but yesterday was the third Thursday, you are a week early.

A calendar makes the second check easy. Count the Thursdays in November and take the fourth one; the following day is Black Friday. If you would rather not count, look at the last Thursday of the month: when November has only four Thursdays, the fourth and the last are the same day, and the Friday after it is the one you want.

Why the Date Moves Every Year

Because Thanksgiving is pinned to a weekday rather than a number, Black Friday slides around. The earliest it can fall is November 23 and the latest is November 29, depending on which weekday November 1 happens to be. That is a seven-day window, which is why the date you remember from last year is rarely the date you need this year.

The shift has a practical effect: the number of shopping days between Black Friday and Christmas changes annually, and retailers adjust their schedules accordingly. It also means that a question like "is it today?" has a different answer each year, and no single date can be memorised once and reused.

If you are checking well ahead of time, work backwards from the end of November instead of forwards from the start of the month. The last full week of November almost always contains the date, and the Friday of that week is the one to plan around.

The Answer Depends on Your Time Zone

Black Friday is a date on the US calendar, so two people can answer the question differently on the same day. If you are in Asia or Australia, your Friday may begin many hours before Friday starts in New York. Sales run by US retailers usually follow the retailer's own local time, not yours, so a deal labelled for Friday can go live while it is still Thursday where you are.

Outside the United States, the label is used more loosely. Many retailers in the UK, Europe and elsewhere run promotions under the Black Friday name for a week or longer around late November. Whether today is technically the date matters less there than whether a given sale is currently live.

When a deal page shows a start time, read the time zone attached to it. That single detail explains most of the confusion about whether a sale has begun.

What It Means If Today Is Not the Day

If you are before the date, you are probably already inside a sale window. Many retailers open Black Friday pricing days or even weeks early, and marketing emails start using the label long before the Friday itself. A deal you see today may be the same one that will be advertised later, or it may be an early version with different stock.

If you are after the date, the next notable moment is Cyber Monday, the Monday that follows the Thanksgiving weekend, which is when online-focused discounts tend to peak. Some sales keep running past that point into early December.

Neither case means you have missed everything. It means the date on the calendar and the period when discounts are actually available are two different things, and the second one is usually longer.

Does the Sale Last All Day?

The date lasts twenty-four hours, but the selling rarely fits inside it. In the US, stores have historically opened on Thanksgiving evening or in the early hours of Friday, and online discounts often appear at midnight or shortly before. Doorbuster-style offers are usually limited in quantity, so they can sell out before most people wake up.

Many promotions run through the weekend and end on Cyber Monday, and some extend across the whole week. If today is Black Friday and a particular deal is not live yet, it is often worth checking again later in the day rather than assuming it has been skipped.

Timings vary by retailer and by country, and nothing about the date guarantees a specific launch hour. The safest approach is to check the retailer's own announcement for the exact start time.

FAQ

Is Black Friday always on a Friday?

Yes. By definition it is the Friday immediately after Thanksgiving in the United States, so it can only ever fall on a Friday.

Does Black Friday fall on the same date every year?

No. It moves within a seven-day window, from November 23 at the earliest to November 29 at the latest, because it follows the fourth Thursday of November.

Do Black Friday sales only happen on that one day?

Usually not. Many retailers start discounts days or weeks earlier and keep them running through the weekend, with Cyber Monday following on the next Monday.

Is Black Friday a public holiday?

No. It is not a federal holiday in the United States; it is a shopping day that follows the Thanksgiving holiday.
